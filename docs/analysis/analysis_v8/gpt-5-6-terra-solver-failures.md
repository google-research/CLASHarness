# gpt-5.6-terra — solver failure deep-dive (v8 corpus)

> [!NOTE]
> **Scope.** One model, one corpus. Every figure below is
> `reasoning_effort=high` on the v8 conflict corpus (600 tasks = 60 scenarios
> × 10 rounds) unless a row says otherwise. Cross-model comparisons live in
> [cross-model-behavioral-comparison.md](./cross-model-behavioral-comparison.md);
> the measurement-validity argument, the F1–F6 definitions and the corpus-wide
> difficulty analysis live in
> [eval-results-capability-analysis.md](./eval-results-capability-analysis.md).
> This document does not restate them.

**Provenance.** Served through `bedrock-gpt` as `openai.gpt-5.6-terra`. Every table here is generated by
`tools/emit_solver_failures.py` from `failure_taxonomy.json` and
`cross-model-behavioral-comparison.stats.json` — plus, for section 4.3 only, the
scenario seeds under `clasharness/data/scenario_seeds/conflict_vlm_v1/`, which
supply the bucket each task belongs to but none of its numbers. Every table is
re-verified cell by cell by `tools/audit_report.py` (pass E). No number in this
file was typed by hand.

gpt-5-6-terra sits exactly on the field median for pass@budget at 20.0%, yet ranks 6 of 9 on all three finer-grained measures: avg_score 0.5891 against a median of 0.5909, per-check accuracy 57.9% against 58.3%, and 1,523 failed required and diagnostic units against a median of 1,489. <!-- PROSE-clustering-terra-BEGIN -->

A per-check pass rate of 57.9% would produce only a 7.6% task pass rate if checks failed independently; the observed rate is 20.0%, a lift of 1.36× whose 95% CI of [1.29, 1.44] excludes 1. Failures do concentrate — a given scenario tends to go wholly right or wholly wrong — and at 2.01× the overdispersion is a little above the field median of 1.94×. That 12.4 pp excess is what closes the gap identified in section 1, turning rank 6 on per-check accuracy into rank 5 on tasks. The effect is real but ordinary; it is not a differentiator, only the reason the two rankings disagree.

### 4.3 Designed difficulty levers

Sections 4.1 and 4.2 measure difficulty as it turned out. This one measures it
as it was *designed*: each task is joined to the scenario seed it was generated
from, and grouped by what that seed declares. The two boolean knobs give
non-overlapping arms; the conflict plugins do not. The generator resolves each
seed's `conflict_taxonomy` and `difficulty_challenges` tokens against the plugin
registry and one seed routes to several plugins at once, so those rows overlap
and do not sum to the corpus.

Read the `seeds` column first. Some plugins are carried by a single seed, and a
delta on one of those describes that one scenario rather than the lever, however
large it looks. The corpus-wide version of this table, with all thirteen models side
by side and the levers ranked, is section 5.3 of the capability analysis.

## 5. Interpretation

<!-- PROSE-interpretation-terra-BEGIN -->

gpt-5-6-terra is mid-field for a specific reason: it ranks 6 of 9 on every unit-level measure and 5 of 9 on tasks, with a 1.36× clustering lift doing all the work between the two. The headroom is in finishing, not in perceiving or reasoning — 68.0% of required terminal actions are never emitted, `missing_required_side_effect` is the largest stop reason at 261 tasks, and the action mix pairs above-median `file_write` with below-median `asset_open` and `message_send`, a model that writes something and calls it done. Reasoning effort confirms that diagnosis by failing to move it: grounding drops 84 → 61 → 54 and avg_score reaches 0.6024, but pass@budget is pinned at 20.0% from medium onward. The scenario view agrees at a coarser grain — no catastrophic weakness, worst gap −0.110, yet 12 of 60 scenarios that never once pass. Closing that requires changing what the model emits at the end of a run rather than giving it more room; it already stops early at a mean of 9.98 steps and reaches the cap only 8 times, mostly in a loop.
