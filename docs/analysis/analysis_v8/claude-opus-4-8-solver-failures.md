### 4.3 Designed difficulty levers

Sections 4.1 and 4.2 measure difficulty as it turned out. This one measures it
as it was *designed*: each task is joined to the scenario seed it was generated
from, and grouped by what that seed declares. The two boolean knobs give
non-overlapping arms; the conflict plugins do not. The generator resolves each
seed's `conflict_taxonomy` and `difficulty_challenges` tokens against the plugin
registry and one seed routes to several plugins at once, so those rows overlap
and do not sum to the corpus.

Read the `seeds` column first. Some plugins are carried by a single seed, and a
delta on one of those describes that one scenario rather than the lever, however
large it looks. The corpus-wide version of this table, with all thirteen models side

## 5. Interpretation

<!-- PROSE-interpretation-opus-BEGIN -->

At 23.8% this model is second of nine, and the benchmark is nowhere near solved for it. Its shape is the mirror of what the corpus rewards: rank 1 of 9 on `side_effect` at 59.8%, the mechanical act of emitting the terminal action, but rank 4 on `content_msg` at 54.3%, which is simultaneously the largest absolute bill at 648 required failures and the bucket that separates models. Reasoning effort does not reach it — grounding falls 66 → 48 → 38 across the three settings while pass@budget peaks at medium and drops to 22.8% at high, with step-capped tasks climbing to 37. What converts a third-place per-check accuracy of 60.8% into a second-place task rate is concentration, a 1.42× lift, not breadth; the same accuracy spread evenly would pass far less often. The gating line states the remaining headroom plainly: 396 of 457 failed tasks involve a content check and exactly 1 fails on grounding alone, so the work left is composing the right words into the right artifact, not seeing more or thinking longer.
